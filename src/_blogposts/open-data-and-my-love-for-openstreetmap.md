title: Open data, and my love for OpenStreetMap

It should come as no surprise to anyone that I love open data. As a self-proclaimed hacker[^1], I love being able to create whatever I want with freely available data. I love being able to modify and to help contribute to massive datasets, even if that is only in smaller degrees. The Norwegian government even has a dedicated data page with [over 1600 open datasets](https://data.norge.no/search-all?opendata=true), free for anyone to use.

[^1]: Think the early definition of the word, similar to how the term is used for "hackerspaces" nowadays; Someone who enjoys tinkering and working with computers, not the illegal part of it.

One of the better and larger datasets out there that allow me to explore, use, and contribute data without issue is [OpenStreetMap](https://www.openstreetmap.org/). It works as the backend for *numerous* other services, including some of my favourites:

- [MapComplete](https://mapcomplete.org/) - An easy site to view and add specific types of data, including hackerspaces, sports facilities, public toilets, defibrillators, surveillance cameras, and much more.
- [StreetComplete](https://streetcomplete.app/) - Makes it easy to contribute missing data to OSM, with a simple app-based interface.
- [Wheelmap](https://wheelmap.org/) - Helps users find and contribute wheelchair accessible places.

I use all of these frequently to help improve the quality of the OSM data, as its such an easy way to contribute. I highly encourage everyone who has the time to do so to contribute data whenever they can. It costs nothing, and it improves everyone's lives just a small bit.

I especially encourage everyone to contribute accessibility information, as this can help anyone in wheelchairs, anyone with crutches, with sensory issues, or really anyone at all to have a better time going outside in a world not made for us.
